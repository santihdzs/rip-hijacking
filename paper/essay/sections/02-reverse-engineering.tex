\section{Reverse-Engineering the RIP MD5 Authentication}\label{sec:reveng}
\paragraph{Packet layout.} Each captured update is a RIPv2 \emph{Response}
(command~2) sent to the multicast group 224.0.0.9 on UDP/520. Authentication of
type~3 (Keyed Message Digest) places an authentication \emph{header} as the
first route-table entry and appends an authentication \emph{trailer} carrying
the digest. The observed size-06 packet decomposes as in \autoref{tab:layout}.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\footnotesize
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l l X}
\hline
\textbf{Region} & \textbf{Bytes} & \textbf{Contents (size-06 capture)}\\
\hline
RIP header        & 4  & command=2, version=2, routing-domain=0\\
Auth header (RTE) & 20 & AFI=0xFFFF, auth-type=3, pkt-len=44, key-id=1, auth-len=20, seq, reserved\\
Route entry       & 20 & the advertised route\\
Auth trailer      & 20 & AFI=0xFFFF, type=0x0001, then the 16-byte digest\\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\caption{RIPv2/RFC~2082 authenticated packet layout (64-byte payload).}
\label{tab:layout}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Digest construction (the key finding).} The assignment brief names
``HMAC-MD5'', but the captured trailers are \emph{not} reproducible with HMAC.
RFC~2082 instead specifies a \emph{keyed} MD5: the 16-byte digest field is
first overwritten with the key, and a plain MD5 is taken over the whole packet.
Concretely, writing $D$ for the payload bytes from the RIP header through the
trailer's \texttt{AFI}/\texttt{type} field (i.e.\ everything except the 16 digest
bytes) and $K_{16}$ for the ASCII key right-padded with zero bytes to 16:
\begin{equation}
\text{digest} \;=\; \mathrm{MD5}\bigl(\, D \,\|\, K_{16} \,\bigr).
\label{eq:keyed}
\end{equation}
We tested both constructions and both candidate alphabets by brute force; only
\eqref{eq:keyed} over a \textbf{base-36} alphabet (lowercase letters and
digits, \emph{not} hexadecimal) reproduces the captured digest.

\paragraph{Recovered key and validation.} The single-threaded oracle recovers
\texttt{ykusrq} (index $1{,}618{,}816{,}210$ of $36^6$). Validation is not a
single-packet coincidence: the same key reproduces the digest of
\textbf{all 21} authenticated packets in the capture, each with a distinct
sequence number and payload. \autoref{tab:reveng} summarizes the corrected
parameters.
