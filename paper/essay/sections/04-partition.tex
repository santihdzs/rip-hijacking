\section{Keyspace Partition Strategy}\label{sec:partition}
\ph{Main idea: the keyspace is indexed and split into fixed-size chunks served
from a queue. Then, in order: the search space is every length-6 string over the
base-36 alphabet, of size $N = 36^6 = 2{,}176{,}782{,}336$; each integer index
$i \in [0, N)$ maps to a key by little-endian positional encoding, so a contiguous
index interval is a set of distinct keys with no overlap between intervals; the
master divides $[0,N)$ into fixed-size chunks of $C$ keys, giving
$\lceil N/C \rceil$ chunks (e.g.\ $C = 5\times10^6$ yields 436 chunks) served from
a queue. Close on: contiguous non-overlapping intervals are what make dynamic
assignment and safe re-queue possible.}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\footnotesize
\caption{Dynamic, non-overlapping assignment}
\begin{algorithmic}
\STATE pending $\leftarrow$ queue$(0,1,\dots,\lceil N/C\rceil-1)$;\; inflight $\leftarrow \emptyset$
\WHILE{a \texttt{REQUEST} arrives and not found}
  \IF{pending $\neq \emptyset$}
     \STATE $c \leftarrow$ pop(pending);\; inflight$[c] \leftarrow$ (worker, now)
     \STATE send \texttt{ASSIGN}$(c,\, cC,\, \min((c{+}1)C, N))$
  \ELSIF{inflight $\neq \emptyset$}
     \STATE send \texttt{NOWORK} \COMMENT{outstanding chunk may be requeued}
  \ELSE
     \STATE mark exhausted;\; broadcast \texttt{STOP}
  \ENDIF
\ENDWHILE
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\ph{Main idea: why dynamic pull-based assignment beats a static split and how it
composes with failure recovery. Then, in order: pull-based assignment keeps
heterogeneous or contended workers balanced automatically rather than fixing
shares up front; a chunk is only ever ``done'' when its \texttt{DONE} arrives; so
a lost chunk can be safely re-queued (\autoref{sec:death}). Close on: assignment
and recovery share one invariant, a chunk is complete only on \texttt{DONE}.}
