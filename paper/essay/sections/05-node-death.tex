\section{Node-Death Detection and Reassignment}\label{sec:death}
\ph{Main idea: no assigned chunk may be silently lost, and the master defends
against two failure modes by requeueing to the front of pending. Then, in order:
the risk is that a lost chunk lets the search terminate reporting ``not found''
while skipping the key; hard crash or kill, where the TCP socket closes, the
master's read returns EOF, and the disconnect handler requeues every chunk that
worker held; freeze or network partition, where the socket stays open but
\texttt{PROGRESS} heartbeats stop, and a monitor thread requeues any chunk whose
last heartbeat is older than a timeout $T$ (default $5$\,s) and marks the node
dead. Close on: both modes requeue to the front of \texttt{pending} so a
surviving worker picks the chunk up.}

\paragraph{Empirical check.} \ph{Main idea: a controlled fault-injection run
confirms no chunk is lost. Then, in order: with three workers, freeze one with
\texttt{SIGSTOP} and kill another with \texttt{SIGKILL}; the master emits the
expected events shown below, a timeout requeueing chunk 19 and a disconnect
requeueing chunk 40; both requeued chunks are subsequently completed by the
surviving worker. Close on: coverage of the keyspace stayed total, so neither
failure mode drops a chunk.}
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\ttfamily
TIMEOUT: worker freeze-w1 silent >4.0s, requeued chunk 19\\
worker kill-w2 disconnected; requeued chunks [40]
\end{quote}
