\section{TCP Coordination Protocol and Message Types}\label{sec:protocol}
The master and workers communicate over raw TCP sockets using a line-oriented
protocol: each message is one JSON object terminated by a newline, which makes
framing trivial and the traffic human-readable during debugging. The master
never blocks a fast worker: chunks are handed out \emph{on request}, so faster
nodes simply request more often. \autoref{tab:msgs} lists the message types.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\footnotesize
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l l X}
\hline
\textbf{Message} & \textbf{Direction} & \textbf{Purpose / fields}\\
\hline
\texttt{HELLO}     & W$\rightarrow$M & join; worker id, host, pid, cores\\
\texttt{PARAMS}    & M$\rightarrow$W & target data, digest, alphabet, length, construction\\
\texttt{REQUEST}   & W$\rightarrow$M & ask for a keyspace chunk\\
\texttt{ASSIGN}    & M$\rightarrow$W & \{chunk id, start, end\}\\
\texttt{PROGRESS}  & W$\rightarrow$M & \{chunk, tried, rate\}; doubles as a heartbeat\\
\texttt{HEARTBEAT} & W$\rightarrow$M & liveness while idle between chunks\\
\texttt{DONE}      & W$\rightarrow$M & chunk exhausted with no match\\
\texttt{FOUND}     & W$\rightarrow$M & \{chunk, key, index\} on a match\\
\texttt{NOWORK}    & M$\rightarrow$W & queue momentarily empty; retry\\
\texttt{STOP}      & M$\rightarrow$W & global halt (key found, or keyspace exhausted)\\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Master--worker message protocol.}
\label{tab:msgs}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Lifecycle.} A worker sends \texttt{HELLO}, receives \texttt{PARAMS},
then loops: \texttt{REQUEST}~$\rightarrow$~\texttt{ASSIGN}, crack the chunk in
sub-batches while emitting \texttt{PROGRESS}, and report \texttt{DONE} or
\texttt{FOUND}. On \texttt{FOUND} the master \emph{re-verifies} the claimed key
against the captured digest before trusting it, then broadcasts \texttt{STOP} to
every connected worker so no node keeps hashing. The master node is never idle:
it launches local worker subprocesses that connect back over loopback and crack
alongside the remote workers.
