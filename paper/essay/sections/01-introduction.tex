\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\ph{Main idea: motivation and threat model for attacking RIPv2 authentication.
Then, in order: RIPv2 distributes routing updates with only optional
authentication, and the RFC~2082~\cite{rfc2082} keyed message-digest mode is the
sole barrier to a forged update; an on-path attacker who forges an accepted
update can inject or override routes to blackhole or man-in-the-middle traffic;
this work stays honest to the lab scope by doing offline key recovery from the
provided captures, not a live attack. Close on: the recovered key is what would
unlock a forged update, with live injection left as a documented stub
(\texttt{inject\_route.py}).}

\ph{Main idea: what this report does and the roadmap through the sections. Then,
in order: reverse-engineer the exact digest construction from a capture
(\autoref{sec:reveng}); build a master--worker cluster that partitions the
keyspace across TCP-connected workers (\autoref{sec:protocol},
\autoref{sec:partition}); tolerate node failure so no chunk is left unprocessed
(\autoref{sec:death}); characterize throughput (\autoref{sec:bench}). Close on:
the captures use six-character base-36 keys, recoverable in minutes at the rates
we measure.}
